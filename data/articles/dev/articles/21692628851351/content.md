<h2 id="h_01HRAK07HDK488A5ETZN13TNV1">Introduction</h2>
<p>If you’re reading this, you were likely invited to test a private Activity from a developer.</p>
<p><img src="https://support-dev.discord.com/hc/article_attachments/21864141171607"></p>
<p>Private Activities are intended for small-scale building and testing of new ideas. You should only test apps from developers that you trust. If you were invited to test an app that you don’t recognize, and you think it might be breaking Discord’s rules, you can <a href="https://dis.gd/report-an-activity">report it here</a>.</p>
<h2 id="h_01HRAJS76QWG648YHNFVDWEN17">What are private Activities?</h2>
<p>Discord recently launched a Developer Preview for our Activities platform, meaning that any developer can make their own Activity on Discord. During this preview period, developers can invite people to test what they’re building in small, private servers of less than 25 members, and DMs.</p>
<p>These apps are not built by Discord. However, we will take action on apps we believe are breaking Discord’s rules. If you believe an app you’ve been invited to test might be breaking Discord’s rules, you can <a href="https://dis.gd/report-an-activity">report it here</a>.</p>
<h2 id="h_01HRAJS76Q4ZA6EJ7RMCCE5HY4">Are private Activities safe to use?</h2>
<p>Activities are like websites embedded in Discord, and are owned and operated by other developers. While they are accessible from within Discord, using an Activity is like being on a website outside of Discord.</p>
<p>As with any website, it may be able to access certain information about you, including your IP address. You should refer to the app’s developer or their privacy policy to learn about their data collection and use practices.</p>
<p class="callout">⚠️ You should only test apps from developers that you trust. Discord will never ask you for your password inside an activity.</p>
<h2 id="h_01HRAJS76Q9GWPHWCB7QXJ51F0">What information can a private Activity access?</h2>
<p>Activities are not able to access information about your Discord account without your approval. Apps will ask for your approval by showing you this screen, which tells you what they want to access. Clicking “Authorize” will grant the app access to this information.</p>
<p><img src="https://support-dev.discord.com/hc/article_attachments/21864141185559"></p>
<p>If you ever change your mind, and no longer want the app to access this information about you, you can revoke access by going to <strong>User Settings &gt; Authorized Apps</strong>, and selecting <strong>Deauthorize</strong> on the application.</p>
<p>Some apps may collect non-Discord data when you use them. Refer to the developer or their Privacy Policy for information about their data practices.</p>
<h2 id="h_01HRAJS76QN4328CKA7ES19MTY">I don’t want to use the app anymore. How can I turn it off?</h2>
<p>If you ever change your mind, and no longer want an app you used to access information about you, you can revoke access by going to <strong>User Settings &gt; Authorized Apps</strong>, and selecting <strong>Deauthorize</strong> on the application.</p>
<p>If you were invited to the developer team that owns the app, and you want to remove yourself, you can</p>
<ul>
    <li>Go to the <a href="https://discord.com/developers/teams">Teams page of our Developer Portal</a>
    </li>
    <li>Select the team you were invited to.</li>
    <li>Click <code>Remove</code> next to your own name.</li>
</ul>
<h2 id="h_01HRAJS76QNKD2X2YNW38XAH9F">How do I report an app?</h2>
<p>If you think an app might be breaking Discord’s rules, you can <a href="https://dis.gd/report-an-activity">report it here</a>.</p>
