      <p>
        When you’re hanging out with your friends on Discord, having the option
        to share what you’re up to, no matter where you’re at, helps make your
        friends feel just a bit closer. It’s like turning your phone or laptop
        towards your friend when they’re right next to you!&nbsp;
      </p>
      <p>
        You can use the desktop or mobile app to stream whatever you’re doin’
        faster than the time it took you to read this sentence. (And perhaps
        <em>this</em> sentence too.) Read on to learn how to stream to Discord,
        what the options you’ll see when setting up your stream mean, and which
        PC and mobile platforms you can stream from.
      </p>
    </div>

      <h2>
        <strong
          >How to stream on Discord with your desktop or laptop<br /></strong
        >Table of Contents: Streaming from desktop&nbsp;
      </h2>
      <p>
        You’ve loaded up your favorite game, and now it’s time to show THE WORLD
        you’ve got what it takes. Or, at least show your friends that you can
        hold your own in an online match. Same stakes basically.&nbsp;
      </p>
      <figure
        style="max-width: 1550px"
        class="w-richtext-align-fullwidth w-richtext-figure-type-image"
      >
        <div>
          <img
            src="https://cdn.prod.website-files.com/5f9072399b2640f14d6a2bf4/675b6515caa12a3ba9810b21_AD_4nXfED5iwQ98orVAaACvlWosWoypwYjZKNW67DHQT8dK7iOEOCAqIqOTx99jkPgM1xEf_lkoKEut8clt-LWpvTwv9_caRdbITtYb5de7JSw_GyHjNiAdr5VvfDZ4AniVH0ZknMGw5Qg.gif"
            loading="lazy"
            alt="A demonstration of the process for streaming to Discord."
          />
        </div>
      </figure>
      <p>
        To start streaming using the desktop app, follow the steps below! This
        works on Windows, macOS, and Linux.
      </p>
      <ul role="list">
        <li>
          <strong>Join in any voice call</strong>, whether it’s a channel on a
          server or in a DM.&nbsp;
        </li>
        <li>
          At the bottom of the call window,
          <strong
            >press the button that looks like a computer monitor with an arrow
            on it</strong
          >.&nbsp;
        </li>
        <li>
          <strong>Select the application, screen, or capture device</strong> you
          want to show off.&nbsp;
        </li>
        <li>Adjust your stream quality settings as you’d like.</li>
        <li><strong>Press Go Live</strong>!&nbsp;</li>
      </ul>
    </div>
  </div>
  <div id="heading-3" class="rich-wrapper">
    <div class="blog-post-content w-richtext">
      <h2>
        <strong>How to stream on Discord on the mobile app<br /></strong>
      </h2>
      <p>
        Is the game you’re into the most a mobile game, or perhaps looking for
        some feedback on a drawing you’re making on your tablet? Streaming isn’t
        restricted to your PC… you can also stream to Discord directly from your
        smartphone or tablet if it’s running iOS or Android!
      </p>
      <figure
        style="max-width: 30%"
        class="w-richtext-align-center w-richtext-figure-type-image"
      >
        <div>
          <img
            src="https://cdn.prod.website-files.com/5f9072399b2640f14d6a2bf4/675b6532efae92fa1d53872f_AD_4nXeWBU1xhcrn6dMZXpPjTwSW7THuYCMXuFuOPKRw7S-JGE9ZkeImdvW-AJgz54SBI2K-0BbdwpjYypuSRU0NzPYIRLrhm3710xpzrTzmGGsN3GxBIy5C8lWVav9_wJxujF8V6E2BZA.png"
            loading="lazy"
            alt="The “Share your Screen” button highlighted on the Discord mobile app."
          />
        </div>
      </figure>
      <p>
        The next time you’re in a Discord call on your phone, follow the steps
        below to start sharing what’s on your mobile device’s screen with your
        friends:
      </p>
      <ul role="list">
        <li>
          Use the small tab at the bottom of your screen to view more options.
        </li>
        <li>
          In this new menu,
          <strong>press the Share Your Screen button</strong> that looks like a
          smartphone with an arrow pointing out of it.
        </li>
        <li>
          Last, use your device’s streaming options presented to you to confirm
          you want to share your screen.
        </li>
      </ul>
      <p>
        <strong
          >When streaming from your phone or tablet, EVERYTHING will be
          shown,</strong
        >
        just as you see it on your phone’s screen. This includes your phone or
        tablet’s inbound notifications, so consider temporarily muting your
        notifications so you don’t have anything secret shown.&nbsp;
      </p>
    </div>

      <h2>
        <strong
          >Should I choose “Share Application” or “Share Entire Screen” when
          streaming on Discord? <br
        /></strong>
      </h2>
      <p>
        Well, my friend… that depends on how much you wanna share with your
        server or DM buddies. Did you just meet ‘em, or have they been part of
        your chaotic group chat for years?
      </p>
      <figure
        style="max-width: 1600px"
        class="w-richtext-align-fullwidth w-richtext-figure-type-image"
      >
        <div>
          <img
            src="https://cdn.prod.website-files.com/5f9072399b2640f14d6a2bf4/675b66246f513eaeadfe48a3_AD_4nXeQ_Kt0hmaujvQUaRtfb8NswFCe7AOG277JqcZgM1lVpQgjkm9sO4SUesJBzF8F4ZJFAU0X0AgyZa3WstElYVQa33GvGM06bAanT7vM1lJcwEWtioRpmAL17dtGCly_TumcVtl-Rw.png"
            loading="lazy"
            alt="he screenshare setup screen on the desktop client."
          />
        </div>
      </figure>
      <p>
        Either way, there’s an important distinction between these two options
        when choosing which to use:
      </p>
      <ul role="list">
        <li>
          “<strong
            >Share Application” will only show your friends the application or
            game you’ve selected.</strong
          >
          You can also opt to let your friends hear that specific game or app’s
          audio but <em>not</em> the sounds from any other apps you may have
          open.
        </li>
        <li>
          <strong
            >“Share Entire Screen” will share <em>everything</em> you see and
            hear</strong
          >, including both what’s on your screen and — if you choose to share
          your system audio — all the sounds from your applications. Did you
          hear that notification sound or see what it said? So did your friends
          who are watching.&nbsp;
        </li>
      </ul>
      <p>
        Thankfully, it’s easy to adjust or restart your screen share session if
        you change your mind. If you’re sharing your whole screen but suddenly
        want to hide a surprise you’ve got planned for your friend, just switch
        to sharing just a specific application!&nbsp;
      </p>
      <p>
        ‍<strong
          >On mobile, you can only stream your device’s entire screen</strong
        >, including all your sounds and notifications. It’s all or nothin’!
      </p>
    </div>

      <h2>
        <strong
          >Can I stream my sound on Windows, macOS, or Linux? <br
        /></strong>
      </h2>
      <p>
        Anyone who plays competitive games or rhythm games knows: sound is
        <em>everything</em>, and hearing something can mean the difference
        between getting the jump on your opponents or falling flat on your
        (virtual) face. Let your friends listen to those sounds as well!&nbsp;
      </p>
      <figure
        style="max-width: 1600pxpx"
        class="w-richtext-align-fullwidth w-richtext-figure-type-image"
      >
        <div>
          <img
            src="https://cdn.prod.website-files.com/5f9072399b2640f14d6a2bf4/675b6652b12832cd979f6a40_AD_4nXffSIX3Tkd0rtAv5TSF3TpmXhwEKb8scBl_XqDq3WoXMXDA3FLpw_LJ83duF2R0cm2_cd3luQjptKywfpX0sInOhyim5_Oox6neqT-ezkHUiObOAF0YoblaxIZQ9tN11S57RZfyOw.png"
            loading="lazy"
            alt="Illustration of streaming a game to Discord.&nbsp;"
          />
        </div>
      </figure>
      <p>
        If you’re streaming to Discord <strong>from</strong>
        <strong
          >Windows, the mobile app, or your browser using Chrome, you’ll be able
          to share your application’s sound</strong
        >
        — no need to install anything extra to make it work!
      </p>
      <p>
        If you’re streaming to Discord
        <strong
          >from macOS, you’ll be able to stream your audio as long as you’ve
          updated your Mac to at least macOS Ventura</strong
        >, released in 2022.&nbsp;
      </p>
      <p>
        At the time of publishing, you won’t be able to stream your audio if
        you’re either using a browser that isn’t Chrome or you’re using Discord
        on Linux.
      </p>

      <h2>
        <strong
          >What’s the best quality stream to pick on Discord? <br /></strong
        >Table of Contents: Discord streaming quality&nbsp;

      <p>
        There are a LOT of things you can stream to Discord. Perhaps you’re
        playing through a horror game and need your friend’s encouragement as
        you enter that creaking doorway… or maybe you’re sharing a writing
        project you’ve been working on. (You know, like this blog you’re
        reading.)
      </p>
      <p>‍</p>

        class="w-richtext-align-fullwidth w-richtext-figure-type-image"

        <div>

            loading="lazy"
            alt="The Stream Quality settings."
          />
        </div>
      </figure>
      <p>
        On the desktop app, Discord has a few built-in quality options you can
        pick from to make sure your friends have a good time watchin’ ya.&nbsp;
      </p>
      <ul role="list">
        <li>
          <strong>Smoother Video</strong> will lower your stream’s resolution
          but bump the stream’s framerate to your highest-available
          option.&nbsp;
        </li>
        <li>
          <strong>Better Text Readability</strong> will stream your app at its
          exact resolution but will lower the stream’s framerate. If you’re
          asking a friend for some help with your chemistry homework, this will
          be the best for them to see what you’re working on.
        </li>
      </ul>
      <p>
        If you REALLY want to beef up your stream, consider checking out
        <a href="https://discord.com/nitro">Discord Nitro</a>: one of its many
        membership perks is HD streaming, allowing you to share your game or
        screen at higher resolutions and up to 60fps — perfect for sharing that
        insane rhythm game that only a superhuman could keep up with. (How
        <em>do</em> your hands move like that anyway?)
