**Job Description:**
This position is US based only.
Discord empowers people to create spaces where they can find a sense of belonging in their lives. Millions of people trust Discord to keep their communications secure, private, and out of the hands of evildoers, so security and privacy are necessary to Discord's success.
We are looking for a Security Engineer reporting to the Product Security Engineering Manager to join our team in building a secure and protected platform for Discord's users. If you are an Engineer with a wealth of experience making the secure way the easy way, a deep sense of curiosity, and an endless desire to improve Discord, read on!
What you'll do

Join an engineering team developing and implementing full-stack, user-facing security solutions in Discord to protect hundreds of millions of users
Build security controls tools that emphasize self-service, automation, and performance to help identify and mitigate security flaws.
Improve, operate, and maintain security critical services such as authentication and authorization
Partner with Product Teams to deliver secure features
Identify and design solutions for new user security concerns or weaknesses
Research and implement new security capabilities for the Discord platform

Who you are

You have 5+ years experience securing production applications.
You have 3+ years of experience programming in at least one general purpose programming language (e.g. Python, Rust).
You are comfortable developing on iOS and/or Android
You have experience leading security projects with a cross-functional group.
You have experience operating in cloud-based environments (we use Google Cloud, but experience with other platforms is ok too).
You are comfortable reasoning about the security of complex systems, even if they contain components you aren't familiar with.

Bonus points

Experience programming in at least one systems programming language (e.g. C, C++).
Previous experience working in a development or engineering team
Experience developing, operating, and debugging production systems.
Familiarity with common application vulnerabilities on the platforms Discord ships on (that's all of them).
